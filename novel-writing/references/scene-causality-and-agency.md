# Scene Causality and Agency

Use this file for conflict scenes where viewpoint, authority, expertise, movement, or continuity must produce a legible chain of consequences.

## Contents

- Scene roles and causal spine
- POV pressure, space, action, and dialogue
- Reveal timing and chapter interfaces
- Patch-versus-rewrite decisions

## Separate the Scene Roles

- **Experiential center**: whose perceptions and pressure primarily organize the reader's experience.
- **Problem owner**: who bears the immediate stakes or obligation.
- **Decision owner**: who has actual authority to choose among the available options.
- **Domain actor**: who has the expertise or capability to perform a specialized action.
- **Execution owner**: who coordinates and carries the decision through after the exceptional moment passes.

One character may hold several roles. Do not assign them all to the protagonist by default. A protagonist may notice the danger, a clinician may decide what is medically safe, another person may perform the intervention, and an administrator may resume coordination afterward.

## Causal Spine

Use this chain as a diagnostic scaffold, not a mandatory scene template:

```text
spatial and social state
→ experiential center perceives a change
→ interprets or misinterprets it
→ available options narrow or branch
→ an authorized or unauthorized decision occurs
→ a capable or incapable person acts
→ physical or social consequence follows
→ characters reassess
```

Impulsive action, distributed decisions, deliberate disobedience, omniscient narration, and designed viewpoint transitions remain valid. The test is whether the reader can understand why this action happened now and why this person performed it.

## POV as Pressure

POV controls more than hidden information. It determines:

- what is noticed first
- what is missed under stress
- which uncertainty feels urgent
- what interpretation narrows the next choice
- which social exposure or bodily sensation increases pressure

Do not replace a character's unresolved conflict with a clean moral conversion merely because they move closer to one side or perform one useful action.

## Spatial and Action Topology

Before repairing a complex scene, establish:

- each person's position and reachable objects
- sightlines, hearing, entrances, exits, barriers, and hazards
- who proposes the first action
- what the first attempt visibly proves or fails to prove
- why a more direct alternative is unavailable
- when explanation occurs relative to evidence

Prefer evidence or a failed attempt before explanation when the character has no prior reason to suspect the problem. Do not make someone inspect the exact failure point merely because the author knows it matters.

## Dialogue Adjacency

For each consequential exchange, check:

- whether the reply answers, evades, tests, or misunderstands the previous beat
- whether a new arrival has enough context to respond plausibly
- whether technical detail changes action rather than pausing it
- whether the speaker has authority to make the promise or decision
- whether an intentional evasion has visible motive or pressure

## Reveal and Verification Timing

The ability to verify a fact does not create a reason to verify it now. Ask:

- what the character's current priority is
- what verification would interrupt or endanger
- whether the available audience would believe the result
- whether revealing now creates consequence or only satisfies exposition
- whether confirmation belongs after the crisis, after a transition, or in the next chapter

## Chapter Interface Contract

When directly revising a chapter, inspect the necessary adjacent prose.

**Entry state**

- character positions and ongoing movements
- known, unknown, inferred, and concealed information
- unresolved problems
- injuries, key objects, and relationship state

**Exit state**

- the actual state change
- who now owns the next decision or execution
- what remains unverified
- the action, sound, choice, or consequence handed to the next chapter

End on a real state change. Do not give every pair of characters an additional closing line or artificial hook.

## Patch or Rewrite

Use local repair when the causal spine already works and only an anchor, transition, or response is missing.

Rewrite the full scene when:

- the experiential center changes accidentally
- the wrong person owns the decision throughout
- actions require impossible access or foreknowledge
- dialogue depends on a shared context that never existed
- the exit state cannot plausibly produce the next chapter

Do not preserve the original length at the cost of a broken responsibility or causality model.

## Review Questions

- Through whose pressure does the reader experience this conflict?
- Who bears the problem, who may decide, who can act, and who takes over afterward?
- Which perception or misinterpretation triggers each consequential action?
- Can every movement occur in the established space and order?
- What exact state crosses the chapter boundary?
